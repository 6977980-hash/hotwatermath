## Which official source backs each claim the SERP makes
- 120 °F for homes: CPSC 5098, DOE FEMP (real). "EPA recommends 120" (Mr. Rooter): no EPA source found; not repeated.
- 3–5% saving per 10 °F (This Old House, Hunker): the DOE Energy Saver page it came from now returns 404 (see memory note on energy.gov). Not repeated; CPSC's qualitative "conserve energy and save money" used instead.
- 140 °F burns in ~5 s: A. O. Smith Table 1 (<5 s) and CPSC (6 s). Shown side by side.
- 140 °F for Legionella: CDC (storage above 140 °F in building systems; rentals at or above 120 °F; 130–140 °F kills many germs but raises scald risk). OSHA cited by others; we used CDC.
- 115 °F shower code limit (This Old House): codes.iccsafe.org 403; not repeated.

## What they all miss (our additions)
1. **Full scald table** with A. O. Smith's and CPSC's figures side by side, plus the gas dial letter for each temperature.
2. **CDC's actual words** for homes vs building systems, and the honest point that no single home setting is both scald-safe and Legionella-proof without mixing valves.
3. **How much more hot water a hotter tank gives** with a mixing valve (computed with formulas.js: +14% at 130 °F, +29% at 140 °F), so people stop turning tanks up without valves.
4. **Stacking:** A. O. Smith's warning that tanks can be up to 30 °F hotter than the setting; ASSE 1070 says the thermostat must not be the only safeguard.
5. **Hydrogen warning** after two weeks unused (A. O. Smith).
6. Expansion link: 1.0% vs 1.5% water growth at 120 vs 140 °F (AMTROL table, our expansion calculator).
7. Embedded tank size calculator ("check size before you turn it up").
