## Which official source backs each claim the SERP makes
- 210 °F / 150 psi: Watts spec sheet (real). ANSI Z21.22: Watts and A. O. Smith.
- Drip = high pressure / no expansion tank: A. O. Smith manual (real).
- "Replace every 5 years" (Hunker via InspectApedia): no manufacturer source found; Watts says inspect every 3 years and replace if necessary. Not repeated.
- DIY valve replacement steps (Hunker): contradicted by Watts tag ("repair or alteration ... prohibited") and A. O. Smith ("have a qualified person replace"). We give no steps.

## What they all miss (our additions)
1. **Pressure scale diagram** (SVG): 50–60 psi PRV setting, 80 psi code maximum (A. O. Smith), 112.5 psi = 75% of a 150 psi valve (Watts), 150 psi opening.
2. **Drip vs gush table** with the action for each case and its source.
3. **Why homes are "closed"** (Safe Drinking Water Act backflow devices, A. O. Smith) and expansion of 1.0% / 1.5% at 120 / 140 °F.
4. **Embedded expansion tank calculator** (AMTROL method) and the never-adjusted 12 psi pre-charge trap.
5. **Discharge pipe rules table** from A. O. Smith and Watts (¾ in, 6 in, air gap, copper/CPVC, 30 ft / 4 elbows).
6. Test procedure restated from the valve label and manual, plus Watts' 3-year inspection.
7. Part price range (The Home Depot, $14.50–$33.99) and an honest "no sourced labor figure".
